# The Question Bank

The master interview. Eight blocks, each sized to one voice note (2 to 4 minutes). Every
block explains WHY it exists and HOW to personalize it from the website evidence, then gives
the question shapes. Adapt wording to the trade and to what the site actually says; keep the
intent of every block.

Two standing rules:

- **Pre-fill from evidence.** Wherever the website already answers a question, restate the
  finding and ask for confirmation ("Your site says you've been at this since the 1990s...")
  instead of asking cold. Specifics prove you did the homework.
- **Own-site only.** Never quote third-party directories or competitor research at the
  founder. Ask open questions; let their answers confirm or kill outside claims.

---

## Block 1 — Your story (~3 min)

*Why: origin material and the brand's voice. The refusal question is where the wedge lives.*

1. How long have you been at this? What got you started, and was there a moment you decided
   this was the business? *(anchor with the site's "since..." claim if present)*
2. What do other {trade} outfits around {town} do that you refuse to do?
3. Tell me about one customer or job you still remember. What happened?
4. When a customer thanks you, what do they usually say you did differently?

## Block 2 — The person behind the business (~3 min)

*Why: local customers hire the person, not the company. Values and legacy answers become
positioning; the pre-business dream is often the best origin material.*

5. Be honest: {trade} probably wasn't the childhood dream. What was the plan before this,
   and how did this become the thing you built your life around?
6. What are your values? The two or three things you won't compromise on, in business or
   in life?
7. Outside the work: family, hobbies, what does a good Saturday look like?
8. Ten years from now, what do you want people in {town} to say about you and {business}?

## Block 3 — The services (~2 min)

*Why: exact offering names (spelling gets enforced later) and the hero offering that the
content will sell first.*

9. Your site lists {N} services ({list them from the site}). Which ONE do you most want
   more of? That becomes the spine of the content.
10. {Pick the broadest/vaguest service on their site.} What exactly does it include, and do
    you offer any packages, programs, or services that aren't on the site yet?

## Block 4 — The claims (we only publish what you confirm) (~2 min)

*Why: every number, credential, and promise in future content must trace to a confirmed
claim. Ask open questions so unconfirmed third-party claims surface without being fed.*

11. Your site says "{their insurance/credential wording}". What are we allowed to publish
    beyond that? Coverage amounts, registrations, anything a customer would want to see?
12. Your site says "{their guarantee wording}". What is the guarantee exactly, word for
    word, and what are its conditions?
13. Your site shows "{their review claim}". Is that current, and are you happy for us to
    always quote the live number as it grows?

## Block 5 — How you win work (~2 min)

*Why: this is where reports get their magic. The first-call question IS the top search
query; the final doubt is the objection every money page must answer; price anatomy is
content nobody else publishes.*

14. What's the most common question people ask when they first call, word for word? And
    what do you say back?
15. Think of the moment right before someone says yes. What's the last thing they usually
    say or ask? That final little doubt?
16. What do people get wrong about what a job costs? What does the price really depend on
    that customers never think of?

## Block 6 — Insider knowledge (~3 min)

*Why: the wow block. Seasonality writes the content calendar; the gear story is proof of
craft; the perception question makes founders light up because nobody ever asks it.*

17. Walk me through your year: when does the phone go crazy, when is it quiet, and what do
    you wish people booked earlier than they do?
18. {Pick their most distinctive gear/method/detail from the site.} What made you invest in
    that, and what can you do now that most outfits around {town} can't?
19. After {N} years: what can you tell about a {house/job/customer} in the first two
    minutes that the customer has no idea about?

## Block 7 — Working together (~2 min)

*Why: sets communication and approval expectations before the first deliverable, and
surfaces vendor landmines early.*

20. Text, call, or email? When's the best time to reach you, and how often do you actually
    want updates: weekly note, monthly summary, only when something needs you?
21. When we write something in your name, do you want to read every word before it goes
    live, or approve the first few and then trust us with spot-checks?
22. What has a supplier or vendor done in the past that drove you nuts? Tell us so we can
    do the opposite.

## Block 8 — The customers & the area (~4 min)

*Why: segmentation that prioritizes everything. Easiest = operational fit; whales = revenue
concentration (often a segment their website doesn't even sell to); the dream customer =
the persona the first money pages target. The gap between those three is itself a finding.*

23. Who usually calls you, and is the person who calls the same person who pays? What are
    they worried about before they call?
24. When you lose a job to someone else, who wins it and why?
25. Which customers are the easiest? The jobs that go smooth, pay on time, no drama. What
    do those people have in common?
26. And your whales: the biggest jobs or most valuable regulars. What are they, what makes
    them worth so much, and what would more of them change?
27. If we could fill your calendar with just one kind of customer, who would it be?
    Describe them like you're describing a real person.
28. Your site lists {their service-area towns}. Where do you WANT more work, and anywhere
    you'd rather not go?

## The framing that goes around the questions

Open the document with (adapt the name):

> Hey {founder first name}, {25-30} questions so everything we write sounds like you and
> only claims what's true. Easiest way: record a voice note per section and just talk.
> Sounds like a lot; it's about one coffee's worth of talking. Rambling is perfect, we'll
> sort it out. **Skip anything.** A half answer beats a perfect answer we never get.

Close with:

> That's it. Anything else we should know, especially things you'd never want said in your
> name, add it at the end. Send the voice notes back to {user's first name} whenever they're
> ready. One section at a time is perfect; you don't have to do this in one go.
